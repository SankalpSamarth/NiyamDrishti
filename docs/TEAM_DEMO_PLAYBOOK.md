# NiyamDrishti — Team Pitch & Demo Playbook

Read this once before presenting. Do not try to explain every button.

## The whole product in four words

> **Scan → Check → Connect → Act**

1. **Scan:** Photograph a packaged product and read its declarations using OCR.
2. **Check:** Apply explainable Legal Metrology checks and show the supporting evidence.
3. **Connect:** Identify the same product across stores, e-commerce and older labels.
4. **Act:** Show repeated violations, let the officer verify them and generate a report.

## One-sentence pitch

> NiyamDrishti is an evidence-first compliance intelligence system that scans packaged commodities, checks mandatory declarations and connects the same product across sellers, locations and time to discover whether a violation is spreading.

## What makes us different

Ordinary label checkers answer:

> Is this one label compliant?

NiyamDrishti also answers:

> Where else has this product appeared, what changed, and is the same violation repeating across the market?

That second question is our **Compliance DNA** innovation.

## Recommended four-minute presentation

### 0:00–0:30 — Start with the problem, not the interface

Hold up a packaged product if possible.

Say:

> India has millions of packaged products across shops and e-commerce. Every package can contain multiple declarations—MRP, net quantity, manufacturer or importer, packing date and consumer-care information. Officers currently spend valuable time reading and checking them manually. More importantly, one inspection usually remains an isolated record.

Do not discuss the technology yet.

### 0:30–0:50 — Explain the solution simply

Keep the **Home** screen visible.

Say:

> NiyamDrishti turns this into four steps: Scan, Check, Connect and Act. It reads the label, applies explainable checks, links matching products and converts separate inspections into enforcement intelligence.

Point only to the two choices on the Home screen:

- **Reveal Compliance DNA** — reliable presentation demo
- **Scan your own product** — real upload and OCR

### 0:50–1:20 — Open the prepared inspection

Click **Reveal Compliance DNA**.

Point to:

- Product name
- Compliance score
- Passed checks and potential violations
- Location and inspector

Say:

> This is a prepared evidence case so the stage demonstration is reliable. The package has been evaluated by twelve deterministic checks. The system suggests potential violations; it does not issue a legal verdict.

Do not open every finding.

### 1:20–2:20 — Reveal the X-factor

Click **Reveal product network**.

Pause for two seconds and let the judges see the screen.

Say:

> Here is our differentiator. The barcode gives this product a Compliance DNA. NiyamDrishti has linked the current package with an e-commerce listing, another retail sighting and a previous label.

Move from left to right:

1. Point to **Linked product sightings**.
2. Point to **Declaration drift**.
3. Point to the **Enforcement signal**.

Say:

> The marketplace shows a different MRP. The older label has a different unit price and contains an email that is absent from the current package. Two violation patterns have now appeared three times. Instead of treating four records separately, the system recommends a cross-channel verification.

This is the most important minute of the presentation.

### 2:20–3:10 — Prove that the result is explainable

Scroll to **Explainable findings** and select **Consumer email** or **Unit sale price**.

Point to:

- Observed evidence
- Requirement
- Reason for the flag
- Applicable provision
- Image-linked evidence box

Say:

> We do not ask an LLM to decide whether a package passes. OCR acquires the text, while deterministic checks produce a traceable suggestion. Every finding shows what was observed, why it was flagged and which provision it relates to.

### 3:10–3:35 — Keep the officer in control

Point to **Officer verification**.

Say:

> OCR can be uncertain and legal exceptions exist, so the officer remains the final authority. They can confirm, reject or send a suggestion for manual review. The score updates without hiding the human decision.

You may change one finding to **Send to review** if time permits.

### 3:35–4:00 — Close on impact

Point to **Editable report** and **Print / save PDF**, but do not download unless asked.

Say:

> The result is an evidence-backed inspection report and a reusable product history. At scale, every new scan strengthens the network, helps departments prioritize repeat patterns and reduces time spent on random manual screening.

Finish with:

> Existing tools check one label. NiyamDrishti discovers whether the violation is spreading.

Then stop. Let the judges ask questions.

## Words everyone on the team should use

- Say **potential violation**, not “confirmed offence.”
- Say **decision-support**, not “AI replaces inspectors.”
- Say **twelve-check POC subset**, not “the entire Act is implemented.”
- Say **seeded cross-channel records**, not “we scraped live marketplaces.”
- Say **matching and comparison logic is live**, because that part genuinely runs.
- Say **OCR reads; deterministic rules decide**.

## Likely judge questions

### “Is this just OCR?”

> No. OCR only reads the label. Our rule engine checks declarations, the evidence layer explains every suggestion, and Compliance DNA connects matching products to detect changes and repeated violations.

### “What exactly is innovative?”

> The unit of analysis is not one image—it is the product across channels and time. Compliance DNA reveals MRP conflicts, disappearing declarations and repeated violations across linked sightings.

### “Are these the actual Legal Metrology rules?”

> The cited provisions and declaration categories are real. Our twelve checks are a demonstrative subset and require expert validation, amendment consolidation and exception handling before enforcement deployment.

### “Why not use an LLM for everything?”

> A legal screening result must be reproducible and auditable. We may use AI for OCR and future document assistance, but pass/fail suggestions come from versioned deterministic rules.

### “What if OCR is wrong?”

> Extracted text is editable, confidence is visible, physical measurements can be routed to manual review, and an officer confirms the final decision.

### “Where did your dataset come from?”

> The internal POC uses clearly labelled synthetic demo evidence plus user-uploaded images. Our next phase is a consented, expert-annotated product dataset split at product level to prevent leakage.

### “Can it scale outside India?”

> The OCR, evidence and product-identity layers remain the same. A country-specific versioned rule pack can replace the Indian rule pack, making the architecture reusable for global packaging regimes.

### “What is the impact?”

> Faster screening means inspectors can focus on high-risk clusters instead of random products. Consumers receive better price and quantity transparency, while compliant businesses face fairer competition.

## If the internet or OCR fails

The main demonstration does not require either one.

1. Run the application locally before entering the room.
2. Use **Reveal Compliance DNA**.
3. State that the case is prepared demo evidence.
4. Show live upload/OCR only if judges specifically request it.

## Final pre-demo checklist

- Run `npm install` before presentation day.
- Run `npm run dev` and open `http://localhost:4173`.
- Use a laptop charger.
- Set browser zoom to 90–100%.
- Close unrelated tabs and notifications.
- Open the prepared demo once to warm up the app.
- Keep one real packaged product as a physical prop.
- Assign one person to speak and one person to click.
- Practise the four-minute flow with a timer at least three times.
- Never apologize for seeded data; explain why it makes the demonstration reproducible.
